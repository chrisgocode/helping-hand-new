# Non-display glasses are an audio-only tier

Meta's non-display glasses expose no screen, and the Wearables Device Access Toolkit reserves display access for Ray-Ban Meta Display. Rather than reproduce the display experience with its visuals removed, we treat non-display glasses as a distinct voice-first tier: a guided session on them is a spoken conversation, and the task tree is traversed by voice command rather than by reading a step and tapping. The alternative — one wearable experience with visual elements degraded to audio — makes the audio tier a lesser copy of something it can never be, and couples every display change to an audio consequence.

## Considered options

- **Feature parity between display and non-display glasses.** Rejected: parity forces the audio tier to describe what the display tier shows. A step rendered as text can be scanned and re-read at will; the same step spoken has to be re-requested, so the interaction model differs even when the content is identical. Parity also blocks display-only affordances that have no spoken equivalent.
- **Non-display glasses as an accessory to the phone screen only.** Rejected: audio output plus microphone input is enough for a complete guided session, and requiring the recipient to look at a phone defeats the point of a wearable for hands-busy tasks like cooking or grooming.
- **A single wearable abstraction that hides which glasses are connected.** Rejected: the capability difference is real and the session shape follows from it. The abstraction belongs one level down, at the voice interface, not at the session level.

## Consequences

- **The task engine gains a voice mode, not a second engine.** A guided session is already a temporary traversal that shows one actionable task at a time. Voice mode changes how the current actionable task is presented and how traversal is requested; it does not change the traversal itself, task identity, or the fact that ending a session clears its position.
- **Audio output during a session is 8 kHz mono.** The glasses expose their microphone only over Bluetooth HFP, and HFP is mutually exclusive with A2DP. Any session that listens forfeits high-quality playback for its whole duration. We hold HFP for the session rather than flipping per turn, because Meta's own guidance requires waiting roughly two seconds for the route to settle and verifying `.bluetoothHFP` in `currentRoute.inputs` before use; flipping per turn would put that delay between every exchange.
- **Microphone input is narrowband and beamformed.** HFP delivers 8 kHz mono, and the microphone array isolates the wearer's voice while suppressing ambient sound and other speakers. Meta documents this as expected behavior. Speech models benchmarked at 16 kHz do not carry their published accuracy to this input, so command recognition must be validated against recorded glasses audio rather than assumed from vendor figures.
- **Command vocabulary must tolerate misrecognition.** Short commands on a narrowband channel produce false accepts, and a false accept on "done" advances the session past a step the recipient has not performed. We make reverse traversal immediate and unremarkable rather than gating forward traversal behind confirmation, because a recipient following a routine should not have to confirm ordinary progress.
- **The audio tier does not depend on the Device Access Toolkit.** For audio the glasses are an ordinary HFP headset, reachable through the platform Bluetooth stack. Toolkit publishing is restricted to select partners during Developer Preview with no announced open-publishing date, so the audio tier can ship on its own schedule while camera and display work waits on Meta.
- **The toolkit is excluded from autolinking while no tier uses it.** Its Meta frameworks arrive as Swift packages that end up referenced twice, at the workspace and under the `EMWDAT` pod target, and an archive then tries to collect two copies of the same `.xcframework-ios.signature` into one folder and fails. Excluding it is not a fix for that; it removes a dependency nothing currently calls, and the duplicate reference has to be resolved before camera or display work resumes.
- **Glasses battery bounds session length, not day length.** Meta rates Gen 2 at roughly 5.5 hours of continuous call audio and Gen 1 lower. A session holding HFP draws against that budget for as long as it runs, which is acceptable for task-length sessions and is not acceptable for anything ambient.

## Revisit when

Meta exposes microphone or audio as a first-class toolkit capability, raises HFP above 8 kHz, or opens display access beyond Ray-Ban Meta Display. Any of those changes the capability gap this decision is drawn around.
